# Getting Started and Building Your Life System

> Classification: Canonical user guide
> Audience: New and restructuring users
> Capability status: Current workspace
> Source authority: [Onboarding](../product/ONBOARDING_SETTINGS_AND_RECOVERY.md) and [Feature Catalog](../product/FEATURE_CATALOG.md)
> Last verified: 2026-08-14

## Build the minimum useful system

During onboarding, choose the change you want first, name the friction LifeBoard should work around, and select only the life areas carrying real responsibilities now. The choices are Work & Career, Life Admin, Health & Self, Relationships, Learning & Growth, Creativity & Fun, and Money. You can rename, archive, reorder, or refresh the resulting Life Map later in Settings → Life Management.

Use this hierarchy:

`life area → project or goal → task, habit, routine, or tracker → day/week placement → evidence and review`

- A **life area** is a continuing responsibility, such as Health & Self.
- A **project** has an outcome and usually ends, such as Move apartment.
- A **goal** describes measurable direction, such as Complete a 10 km training plan.
- A **task** is a concrete action; a **habit** repeats; a **routine** is an ordered/branching run; a **tracker** records evidence.
- A **section** groups project work; a **tag** cuts across the hierarchy.

## Complete onboarding

1. Choose the desired change and up to two recurring frictions.
2. Select two to five life areas and drag them into seasonal priority order.
3. Confirm working hours, weekend availability, and week start.
4. Tune the recommended Plan & Focus, Routines & Health, Reflection & Growth, and EVA connections.
5. Optionally route one real thought through Universal Input, then review its type and destination before saving.
6. Inspect the assembled Life Map and enter LifeBoard immediately, or continue into optional permissions and EVA setup.

Onboarding saves progress. If interrupted, the v4 draft resumes at the exact step. LifeBoard creates no sample chores or fake activity; only a capture you explicitly review may create a record. Calendar, notifications, Health, and EVA remain optional and resumable.

## Configure the daily environment

- In Plan & Organize, set week start, working hours, and timeline anchors.
- Select calendars you actually need. Calendar events are fixed context; LifeBoard does not rewrite them.
- Choose reminder quiet hours and morning/evening rituals.
- On Home, pick Smart, Work, Personal, or Low Energy mode. Add only cards you will consult; adjust size and placement, and let Smart Slots help without surrendering control.
- If using EVA, choose a model/runtime and inspect memory/context controls. Remote context is never a blanket permission.

## Capture without stopping to file

Use the persistent composer, capture sheet, widget, Siri, share extension, or Watch. Type or dictate ordinary language. LifeBoard may deterministically recognize a command, parse task fields, use semantic classification, request clarification, or fall back to EVA. Review the destination editor before saving.

Capture kinds include task, habit, journal, note, tracker entry, mood/energy, hydration, medication event, routine run, and time block. If uncertain:

- action you can finish → task;
- reference you will reuse → note;
- lived experience or private reflection → journal;
- repeated behavior → habit or routine;
- measured evidence → tracker.

## First-week checklist

- Process Inbox at least once; assign a project/area or intentionally leave an item Anytime.
- Run one Focus session and write a brief outcome.
- Complete or correct one habit occurrence.
- Use Repair once, even on a small overload, so recovery feels normal.
- Close one day and review one week.
- Remove one unhelpful reminder, card, or starter item.

## When the structure is wrong

Open Settings → Life Management. Move projects between life areas, create nested projects/sections, retag tasks, and archive inactive structures. Archiving preserves history. Before deletion, read the consequence for children, links, and evidence; use Undo when offered. Shared/team project ownership is not a current capability.
